The fastest way to reach us is to call (760) 769-1159. There is no online form, because water damage is better handled in a conversation. A person answers, asks a few questions and arranges the next step.

Our office is at 72253 29 Palms Hwy in Twentynine Palms. The office address is for reference, since crews come to you.

What to say when you call

A few details help dispatch plan the response.

  • Your town and street, so crews can plan the drive.
  • Where the water came from and whether the source is stopped.
  • What is wet, such as carpet, drywall, hardwood or a crawl space.
  • How long it has been wet, even if you are only estimating.
  • Whether you see sewage, mold or anything near electrical outlets.

Before you call

If a supply line is leaking, shut off the main water valve. Breakers for wet rooms are worth flipping only when the route to the panel is dry. For sewage, electrical or gas danger, call 911 or the utility first, then call us.

Read the emergency water removal page for safety steps, or the mold remediation page if your concern is mold rather than active water.

Finding our office

The office sits on 29 Palms Highway, which is State Route 62 through town, at 72253 29 Palms Hwy in Twentynine Palms. The map on this page is centered on that address. Crews do not work from the office counter, so call before you drive over, especially in the evening when the building may be closed.

If you are outside the city, tell dispatch which community you are in. Each neighboring town has a page of its own covering local conditions, for example Joshua Tree or Morongo Valley, and crews reach Yucca Valley and Landers from the same Twentynine Palms base.

Email and written follow-up

Email goes to contact@twentyninepalmswaterdamagepros.com and is better for sending photos of damage after you have already called than for starting an emergency. Photos of the source, the wet area and any labels on damaged equipment help document the loss.

For an active leak, flooding or sewage, do not wait on email. The phone line is the only channel monitored around the clock, and it is the fastest path to getting crews arranged and to hearing how scope and price will be explained before work starts.

What happens after you call

Dispatch records the address, the source and the affected materials, then explains the next step and what to do while you wait. If crews are available you may get same-day response, and we say plainly when they are not. On arrival the crew maps what is wet using meters and a thermal camera, then lays out the job and its price for you ahead of any work.

Afterward you receive documentation of readings and photos that you can pass to your insurer. The water mitigation page explains the drying stage in detail if you want to know what the equipment in your home is doing.
