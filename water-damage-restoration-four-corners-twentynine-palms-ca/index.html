Water Damage Restoration in Four Corners, Twentynine Palms, CA

Water extraction, drying and cleanup guidance for homes and small businesses near the crossing of Highway 62 and Adobe Road.

Four Corners is the name the Twentynine Palms Historical Society uses for the crossing where State Route 62, also called Twentynine Palms Highway, meets Adobe Road. The society keeps a photograph of the intersection from around 1948, so the name is not new. This page is for people with water damage in a home or small business near that crossing, in the city covered by our main page on water damage restoration in Twentynine Palms.

No official boundary for Four Corners turned up in the research behind this page, so it describes the area loosely as the properties near that intersection. If you are not sure your address counts, call and describe your street. The call is about the water problem, not a map line.

Why a busy crossing sees mixed kinds of water damage

Around a main crossing you usually find shops, offices and houses within a few minutes of each other. That mix means one week can bring a failed supply line in a shop back room, a leaking water heater in a house and a roof leak after a thunderstorm. The cause changes, but the first questions stay the same. Where is the water coming from, how long has it been there, and what has it soaked?

Clean water from a supply line or tank is the easiest case. Water that came in from outside during a storm carries soil and debris, and water from a drain or toilet carries sewage. Those cases need different cleanup, which is why the first thing a call-taker asks is where the water started.

Adobe Road, monsoon runoff and what the record shows

Local news reports show that Adobe Road has taken serious water before. Z107.7 reported that water ran curb to curb on Adobe Road during an August 2013 storm, and that drivers needed rescue at Adobe and Bagley roads in July 2014. After the July 2024 monsoon storms, stretches of Adobe Road stayed closed for days.

A San Bernardino County notice filed with the state says the July 2024 storms caused flooding that damaged roads and flood control facilities in Flood Control District Zone 6, primarily in Twentynine Palms. A Los Angeles Times report said many affected residents lived outside flood zones and had no flood insurance.

None of these reports says the Four Corners intersection itself flooded, so this page does not claim that it did. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Residents who want a property-level answer can look up their own address on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center or ask the city.

Safety steps for the first hour

These steps follow general CDC and EPA guidance on floodwater and sewage, which advises staying out of contaminated water and discarding soaked porous items such as rugs and mattresses.

  • Keep people and pets out of standing water until you know the power to that area is off. Do not touch outlets, cords or appliances while you or the floor are wet.
  • If water is still running from a pipe or tank and the shutoff valve is dry and easy to reach, close it. Otherwise leave it alone and call.
  • Do not flip breakers while standing in water. If the panel is wet or the room is flooded, call your electric utility or an electrician.
  • Treat water from a storm, a toilet or a drain as contaminated. Keep children away and do not try to clear it with a household vacuum.
  • Photograph the damage before moving anything, if that is safe.

How extraction and drying work

Restoration starts with stopping the source and pulling standing water out with pumps or extraction units. A crew then typically checks walls, floors and ceilings with moisture meters and thermal cameras to find wet material you cannot see. Wet carpet pad, insulation and the bottom of drywall are often removed, because they hold water and dry slowly.

Drying uses air movers and dehumidifiers, with moisture readings repeated on later visits until the materials reach a dry target. The industry reference for this work is ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard for professional water damage restoration. It describes how the work is done and is not a credential that this page claims for anyone.

Not every material can be saved. Porous items that sat in contaminated water are usually discarded, while hard surfaces can often be cleaned and dried. A restoration professional should tell you which is which at your property.

Insurance and documentation

The California Department of Insurance says homeowners policies generally do not cover flood damage, though they may cover other kinds of water damage. Sudden and accidental water damage is treated differently from damage caused by slow seepage or leaks. Coverage depends on the cause and your policy, so read the policy and ask your insurer.

The Department also advises photographing damage, keeping records of cleanup and repair costs, making temporary repairs to limit further damage and holding off on permanent repairs until the adjuster has seen the loss. Moisture readings and a written scope from the drying work can add to your records. Nothing here promises that a claim will be approved.

Mistakes to avoid after water enters a building

  • Waiting to see if it dries on its own. Wet drywall and insulation can stay damp behind a dry-looking surface.
  • Running fans or air conditioning in a flooded room before the electrical hazards are checked.
  • Throwing damaged items away before photographing them.
  • Starting permanent repairs before the structure is dry and the insurer has looked at the loss.

Four Corners water damage questions

Where is Four Corners in Twentynine Palms?

The Twentynine Palms Historical Society identifies Four Corners as the intersection of Highway 62 and Adobe Road. No official boundary was found, so treat it as the area around that crossing.

Has the Four Corners area flooded before?

Local news reports describe flooding on Adobe Road in 2013, 2014 and 2024, but none says the Four Corners intersection itself flooded. FEMA zone status for this area was not verified, so check your own address with FEMA or the city.

Does homeowners insurance cover storm water damage?

The Department of Insurance says homeowners policies generally exclude flood, which is separate coverage, though they may cover other water damage. Your policy and the cause of the water decide it, so ask your insurer before assuming.

What should I do first if water is coming into my building?

Get people and pets away from standing water, shut off the water supply only if the valve is dry and easy to reach, stay away from electrical equipment and call for help. Take photos when it is safe.

Can I dry a flooded room with household fans?

Fans alone rarely dry wet walls and floors, and they can spread contamination if the water was dirty. For storm, drain or sewage water, professional extraction and drying is the safer route.
