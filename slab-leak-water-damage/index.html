What a slab leak is, and what we can honestly say about it

A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs in or beneath a concrete floor slab. Water finds the easiest path, so it may rise through cracks and joints, soak into the edges of the slab, or travel sideways under flooring and into the bottom of walls.

We have no verified count of how many local homes sit on a slab, and we will not guess. Many desert homes are built on a concrete slab, but the useful question is specific to your house. Ask your plumber whether your supply lines run under the slab or through the attic and walls.

Safety steps in the first hour

Find the main shutoff and close it. The 2022 LAFCO service review names the Twentynine Palms Water District as the retail water provider here, so your meter and bill come from the district. If you cannot close the valve, ask the district what to do next.

Keep out of any room where water meets outlets, appliances or the panel, and cut power only from a panel you can reach on dry ground. A gas smell, sparks or a buzzing outlet mean walking out and phoning 911 or the utility. Hot water can scald, so treat a warm puddle with care.

Plumber first, restoration second

Drying does not work while pressurized water keeps arriving. The plumber's job comes first, and ours follows or overlaps once the supply is off.

  • Plumber: locates the leak, then chooses a repair such as a spot fix, a reroute around the slab or lining the pipe.
  • Restoration crew: extracts water, removes flooring and wet baseboards, dries the subfloor and walls, and documents readings.
  • Both: agree on where the floor must be opened so that the plumber's access and our drying do not undo each other.

How crews dry a floor that sits on concrete

A crew starts by tracing where the moisture has traveled, using meter readings plus a heat-sensing camera, and then pulls up any standing water. Wet pad comes out, and lifted laminate or tile may come up so air can reach the slab. Air movers push dry air across the floor while dehumidifiers pull water from the room.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is a published reference on water categories and goals for drying, and a fair thing to ask any company about. Fresh supply water leaves more to save, while water that sits for days can pick up contamination and change what can stay.

Walls get the same attention. Crews check the bottom of drywall and cabinets for wicking and open them where readings stay high. Rebuilding finishes after drying falls under rebuild and flooring repair, and day-one work follows water mitigation steps.

A general note on insurance

In the state insurance department's homeowner guide, timing is the dividing line. Sudden and accidental damage is covered, while seepage or leaks generally are not. A slab line that failed suddenly may be judged differently from one that seeped for months. Policies also vary on the cost of reaching the pipe, so ask whether opening the floor is included.

Plumbing leaks are not flood damage, so flood coverage does not apply. The plumber fixes the source, and a written finding helps a claim. State insurance tips also say to photograph damage, keep cleanup records, make temporary repairs and wait for the adjuster before permanent repairs.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency slab leak water damage cleanup available in Twentynine Palms?

Yes, day or night. Our line sends a crew the same day when one is open, but an arrival time is never promised. Close the main valve and stay clear of wet outlets while you wait.

How do I tell a slab leak from a leak in a wall or the attic?

You cannot be certain without a plumber's testing. Warm floor spots, a moving meter and damp flooring in the middle of a room point downward, while ceiling stains point upward. A plumber uses listening and pressure tests to confirm.

Should I call the plumber or the restoration company first?

Close the main valve, then call a plumber, because the source must be stopped. Call dispatch in the same hour if water has reached flooring or walls, since drying can start while the plumber works.

Can the water district tell me whether I have a leak?

The district reads the meter and issues your bill, so its records can show unusual use. Ask the district where its responsibility ends and yours begins, and take your own meter photo as well.

Will I have to tear out all my flooring?

Not always. Clean water caught quickly may leave tile or sealed flooring in place, with drying through lifted edges. Carpet pad, laminate and wet baseboards are more often removed.
